Practical AI
Training

A practical program with three courses by role, helping participants use generative AI safely while following rules.

Three courses: Introductory -> Executive -> Practical Work (Copilot)

Course 01 - Introductory

Introductory Generative AI Training

A foundation course for first-time users to adopt generative AI in work safely and confidently while following rules.

01

Differences between ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, and Claude

ChatGPT is useful for ideation, writing, and consultation; Copilot connects with M365 such as Outlook and Teams; Gemini works well with Google Workspace.

02

What generative AI can and cannot do

Can do: ideation, email drafts, summaries, meeting-note organization, plans. Cannot do: final legal or medical judgments, accurate confirmation of latest or confidential information.

03

Writing emails and replies

Polite client replies, scheduling emails requiring confirmation, apology, scouting, and follow-up emails.

04

Summarizing minutes and notes

Condense long meeting notes, extract key points, and create the next meeting agenda.

05

Planning and ideation

New product and service ideas, multiple social-post ideas, taglines, and promotional copy by target audience.

06

Using Excel and PowerPoint

Creating tables and charts in Excel and slides in PowerPoint.

07

Information that can and cannot be entered

Boundaries for personal information, contracts, estimates, invoices, IDs, passwords, API keys, and member numbers.

08

Following internal rules

Define approved AI tools, use business modes, consult responsible owners on important matters, set input criteria, and understand free versions as personal-use tools.

Course 02 - For Executives

Generative AI Adoption and Use Training for Executives - Copilot Edition

A course for designing generative AI use from a decision-maker perspective, covering adoption decisions, internal rules, data-leakage prevention, and ROI.

01

The current state of generative AI executives should know

Why companies should consider internal use now, the impact on management, and differences between ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, and Claude.

02

Work that may no longer need outsourcing

Simple designs, internal manuals, flyers, training materials, minutes, simple websites, logos, and business cards. It also helps address hiring difficulties.

03

Using AI to address labor shortages

Reduce routine work, shorten email writing and replies, automatically organize minutes and ToDos from meeting notes, and lower workload and turnover risk.

04

Work where adoption effects appear quickly

Complaint responses, apologies, sales emails, presentation materials, inquiries, and sales preparation.

05

Work where AI should not be used

Final legal judgments, personnel evaluations, and medical judgments.

06

How to have members use it

What all members should use, when to start with selected departments, and whether screenshots are allowed.

07

How to prevent information leakage

Free AI use, personal and customer information, SharePoint permissions, and handling confidential documents.

08

Creating internal rules for generative AI use

Approved AI tools, prohibited actions, review flow, responsible owners, and checks before external submission.

09

Adoption roadmap

Start with executives -> one department -> measure effects -> establish rules -> company-wide rollout.

10

How to think about ROI

License costs, reduced labor and outsourcing costs, shorter work time, and improved work quality.

11

Traits of companies that fail with AI adoption

No ownership, no rules, no responsible person, no education, and no permission management.

12

Work that increases in the AI era

Quality and standards checks for AI outputs, second opinions on AI-generated work, and confirming context customers cannot fully communicate to AI.
